## Quality Visuals by AethDesign

This is a starting baseline for frontends and landing pages, not an exhaustive design system. Judge information, composition and usability, not whether a design was human- or AI-generated. User-approved brand and product requirements govern deliberate exceptions; do not invent an exception to retain filler.

### Content and composition

1. Give each page a specific audience, purpose and primary task. Explain the actual offer, service or product and its meaningful distinguishing details. Use real scope, coverage, indicative prices, constraints or evidence when available; do not invent business facts to fill a layout. Task screens prioritize the task rather than marketing copy. Choose each section from the visitor’s question, the useful answer and any relevant next action before choosing its layout. Make the offer or task understandable in the primary hierarchy; a tiny category label must not carry the explanation while the prominent headline stays interchangeable with unrelated businesses. Benefit-led or expressive copy is valid when concrete nearby content makes the offer clear.
2. Every section, sentence and visual treatment must add useful information, support a decision or action, or serve an intentional brand purpose. Remove content whose removal loses nothing useful. Do not default to a hero, reassurance strip, three cards, numbered process, FAQ and closing CTA regardless of the brief.
3. Do not add decorative uppercase slogan eyebrows above headings, illustrations or every section. A category, location or status label is appropriate only when it supplies necessary context. Remove repetitive reassurance, unsupported superlatives, generic slogans and tiny explanatory text that merely restates the heading. Authored UI prose must not introduce em or en dashes; use ordinary punctuation instead.
4. Use arrows and icons for meaningful actions, states or distinctions. Do not append arrows to every redirect or button, or give obvious labels decorative icons by default. Number only an actual sequence, ranking or reference. Do not invent numbered selling points to make a section look designed.
5. Choose the information structure before the container. Default to unboxed content, using typography, alignment, spacing, lists, rows or restrained separators before card grids and filled/bordered panels. A distinct object or clickable action does not itself require a box. Consider text-led actions, image-led entries and integrated field treatments instead of generic rectangles around every button, object or input. Keep controls recognizable, labelled and operable, with visible focus, adequate targets and clear input/error boundaries. Retain containers for a concrete grouping, comparison, interaction-state or product-specific expressive need; keep content and dimensions proportionate. Avoid half-empty boxes, gratuitous bento grids, fake device frames and nested borders used as hierarchy.
6. Use whitespace to group and prioritize without inflating thin content or forcing equal-height cards. Default to an uninterrupted page background; avoid automatic light/dark section alternation, contrasting bands and local backplates. Establish hierarchy on the shared canvas first. Retain a surface change for a specific task/state, required legibility or product-specific visual narrative, with whole-page coherence checked. "Creates separation" or generic contrast is insufficient. Purposeful whitespace and asymmetry remain valid; density alone is not quality.
7. Give each CTA a clear action and an appropriate destination. Repeat it only where the visitor's progress justifies it. Do not add a large closing CTA section, moving slogan band, reassurance bar, FAQ or process panel merely to occupy space. A real process explanation must clarify decisions, timing, requirements or consequences.
8. Establish a coherent, business-specific visual direction through typography, imagery, layout and language. Use a compact system of type, color and spacing roles with deliberate alignment and grouping; inspect actual font loading, line breaks and reading measure. Choose relevant product views, photography, diagrams or intentional artwork for what they communicate. Verify asset rights, subject accuracy, crop/focal point and legibility across sizes; do not present generic or generated imagery as documentary proof. Avoid unrelated illustrations, floating promise badges, excessive italic emphasis and tiny tracked capitals. Decorative assets and motion need a purpose; honor reduced-motion preferences, keep essential content available without animation and do not obstruct operation. A quiet utility or typography-led page can be intentional; do not mandate a signature gimmick, image count, font pairing, animation quota or novelty.

### Design selection and redesign intent

For new frontends and substantial redesigns, establish a short design basis before implementation: audience and primary decisions/tasks, verified product facts and available assets, the concrete product characteristic shaping the hierarchy, and the proposed composition and visual direction. Consider a credible materially different composition and explain the choice against those needs; an obviously unsuitable alternative or adjectives such as premium or modern are insufficient. Keep this proportionate in working evidence, without asking the user to design the page or building multiple full versions. Use bounded assumptions instead of invented business claims. Familiar structures can be the best fit; arbitrary novelty, feature expansion and aesthetic quotas are not required.

A full remake additionally reconsiders the existing information hierarchy, page composition, copy and visual system. Preserve working capabilities, data, permissions, accessibility and explicit brand constraints; existing markup, section order, component shapes and decorative copy are candidates for replacement. Record the current weaknesses and what to remove, merge, reorder, replace or retain. A new palette, font, photograph, border radius or synonym headline alone does not satisfy a full remake. A cleanup or reskin is appropriate when that is the requested scope.

At the first coherent rendered desktop/mobile structure, check the actual content, focal hierarchy, information order and primary task against the design basis before extensive fine styling or evidence collection. Revise an unsupported composition at that point. Record the observed decision briefly; a retrospective rationale alone does not establish that this early check occurred.

For all new/substantially redesigned frontends, review the complete composition as well as components. Look for repeated slogan-led headings, formulaic paired phrases, habitual accent/italic words, tiny category labels, icon tiles, arrow suffixes, equal cards and three-step panels. These are review triggers, not authorship evidence or blanket bans. For retained treatments identify the information, interaction or specific expressive contribution at that location; generic polish, branding, consistency or navigation claims are insufficient. Renaming or restyling filler does not make it useful. Omit ornamental arrows/icons from already-clear labels; keep those communicating a distinct action, state or useful affordance.

For a full remake, compare original/final full-page desktop/mobile views, including task screens. Explain meaningful hierarchy/composition changes and justified retained patterns against the design basis. If the old structure remains with primarily cosmetic substitutions and no product-specific justification, continue redesigning before declaring the remake complete. Do not force useful interactions to change or require unnecessary code churn.

### Product completeness

9. Design mobile composition and content order intentionally, including realistic short, long and missing content, enlarged text and widths where the layout changes. Provide semantic structure, clear form labels, readable contrast, visible keyboard focus, keyboard operation and comfortable touch targets. Keep focus unobscured by sticky content; manage focus on dialog opening/closing and validation, and provide alternatives to gesture-only actions. Respect browser zoom and applicable accessibility criteria rather than importing native-platform dimensions as universal web rules. Prevent clipping, accidental overflow, unreadable supporting text and controls that become cramped at mobile widths.
10. Deliver working navigation and controls with clear loading, empty, validation, error and success states where applicable. Links have real destinations and normal browser link behavior; buttons have real behavior. Preserve relevant navigation state and meaningful entered values after recoverable failures; errors explain the next step. Support appropriate input types, autofill/password managers and paste. Check actual recovery, not only the presence of an error message. A polished happy-path screenshot does not establish a usable frontend. Keep marketing ornament out of login, forms and dashboards when it interferes with the task.
11. Provide accessible, accurate ownership/contact and privacy information and working relevant footer links. Identify the applicable jurisdiction, actual data handling and deployment context before deciding which legal notices or consent controls are needed. Use verified or owner-approved facts and text; never fabricate an address, registration, policy, testimonial, logo, statistic, certification or compliance claim. If required public-launch information is unavailable, report that launch requirement blocked. Clearly label fictional demonstrations without scattering repetitive demo disclaimers throughout the experience.
12. Verify appropriate page titles, metadata, assets, not-found behavior and reasonable loading performance for the actual product. Do not treat a legally incomplete footer, dead controls or missing required states as optional visual polish.

### Visual completion gate

For new pages/frontends and substantial visual redesigns, obtain a fresh independent visual review before claiming completion. Keep the existing code-risk lanes and required verification; low-risk isolated copy/static-style changes still receive one focused builder confirmation unless a stricter requirement or explicit review request applies.

With the toolkit available, declare this scope using `./quality audit-packet --visual --criteria "Observable visual and product outcomes"` and hand the packet and raw changed scope to the fresh reviewer. Instruction-only users provide the same scope, criteria and evidence directly. A packet is not a verdict; omitting a flag does not waive the selected visual gate.

Review representative desktop and mobile renders of every changed screen and relevant interaction/state, including full-page composition and realistic boundary content. Evaluate content usefulness, specificity, hierarchy, typography, spacing, imagery, responsive composition, accessibility, functional completeness and ownership/privacy requirements. A high average score cannot override a blocking finding. Supply the pre-implementation design basis and early composition observation for new/substantial frontend work. Review concept/product fit and full-page composition separately from functional correctness and mechanical compliance; fewer markers or an attractive asset alone do not establish strong design. Full remakes also require original/final renders and an explicit remake-intent conclusion. Missing required comparison evidence blocks that part of the gate.

Classify every observed weakness as an evidence-backed baseline violation requiring correction, a justified treatment with its specific contribution, an optional preference, or missing required evidence marked BLOCKED. Do not leave an identified violation only in scoring commentary. Include the affected screen/viewport/state and evidence; familiarity alone is not a defect. Unjustified filler, repetitive generic layouts, illegible or broken responsive states, dead controls and missing required disclosures/states must be corrected in scope. Re-render and recheck affected states; obtain independent re-review of blocking findings. Missing required render, interaction or independent review evidence is BLOCKED, never a presumed pass. Record remaining preferences and explicitly approved exceptions. Reuse valid passing render/interaction evidence when the source, viewport/state and relevant integration coverage remain applicable. Batch related checks, retain raw evidence by path and summarize results; repeat affected checks after changes or failures rather than gathering unrelated screenshots or regenerating unchanged packets. Do not retroactively rewrite frozen benchmark outputs or scores when this baseline changes.
